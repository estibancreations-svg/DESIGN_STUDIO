# Safety, rights, and lawful disclosure — proposed operating policy

Requested by The Architect on October 3, 2026. Adult-content limits are explicitly pending legal consultation. This is an engineering policy and launch checklist, not a legal opinion or assurance of immunity.

## Non-negotiable platform baseline

- No child nudity, sexualization, CSAM, grooming, trafficking or exploitative child performances. Applies to real, synthetic and stylized subjects and age-ambiguous subjects requiring review. Changing an age field cannot override depicted age or credible evidence.
- Block requested unlawful child activity under the adopted platform rule; contextual/ambiguous depictions go to trained review. Lawful fiction depicting wrongdoing is not automatically illegal. Do not promise a classifier can determine every US federal/state legal issue.
- No nonconsensual intimate imagery, coercive sexual likeness use, fraudulent impersonation or fabricated endorsement. Celebrity hair/eye variations do not establish permission to sell an identifiable replica.
- Real-person identity and voice authorization must specify modifications, performance scope, advertising, distribution, sublicensing, duration and withdrawal handling. A licensed reference photograph is not necessarily a likeness or voice license.
- Separate user age, depicted character age, and audience classification. Adult creators can make lawful children's educational content. Adults and adult settings are not inherently sexual.

## Implemented boundary

The application currently authors metadata and blocking diagrams. It does not accept media uploads, render new media, publish listings, process payments, or forward prompts to a model. Those routes are locked for every user. The metadata regex triage is an advisory early screen; it is not a certified classifier, age estimator, legal detector, or rights determination. Direct draft database access is not media clearance. Draft fields can never authorize production.

## Required before enabling media

Review inputs and generated outputs, including uploaded assets, prompts, speech, selected references and scene context. Use independently validated classifiers, appropriate matching/reporting mechanisms, trusted review and appeal procedures. Fail closed when required checks are unavailable. A moderation decision must pin the exact asset hash, policy revision, reviewer and purpose; edits invalidate it. Render, upload, export and publication gates require trusted review records, rights and age/access status. Provider rules may be stricter than platform policy. No account tier may bypass them.

Store identity documents separately with minimal retention; keep only necessary verification outcomes/references in the app. Do not expose child or incident material in thumbnails, public repositories, diagnostics, regular logs or ordinary moderator queues. Qualified incident handlers must use restricted evidence access. Hash matching requires a qualified, authorized workflow; do not create your own corpus of illegal material.

## Reporting, legal process and privacy

The user requested cooperation with prosecution; the operational term is prosecution, not persecution. Implement an incident-specific legal request process: verify requester and authority; assess jurisdiction and applicable reporting duties; preserve responsive records under an approved hold; require qualified review; disclose only necessary responsive data; record the legal basis, fields released, recipient and time; notify affected users when legally permitted; retain or delete under documented rules. Mandatory reporting can apply separately from a subpoena. Never promise that only one category of request can require disclosure, or that all accounts can be searched without legal basis.

User responsibility belongs in enforceable terms, but it cannot guarantee operator immunity. Liability, federal/state law, safe-harbor applicability, evidence retention, mandatory reports, takedown and appeal deadlines require counsel review. No incident reporting integration or disclosure has been executed here.

## Current legal sources and unresolved applicability

- [18 USC 2258A — provider reporting requirements](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/2258A). Determine applicability, required recipient, preservation and reporting obligations with counsel; this statute is not a generic authorization for unrestricted surveillance.
- [FTC — Complying With the TAKE IT DOWN Act](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-take-it-down-act). For covered platforms, build the required notice/removal process, including applicable 48-hour handling and copy-removal duties. Counsel must assess coverage and exact triggers.
- [FTC — COPPA guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/complying-coppa-frequently-asked-questions). A future child-facing service requires its own privacy/consent design; an adult signup label alone does not resolve child-directed-service duties.
- [US Copyright Office — AI and digital replicas](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/). Copyright, likeness/publicity and voice permissions are distinct.

Sources reviewed October 3–4, 2026. US state laws and international access requirements have not been exhaustively reviewed. Launch adult-account metadata authoring first; keep adult/graphic generation and marketplace release locked pending approved policy and technical controls.
